Combat Rules

Basic Rules, updated November 26, 2026

  • Nonlethal Attacks: can only be made with melee or ranged attacks that require an attack roll, and must be declared before rolling.
  • Cover: Quarter Cover grants +2 AC; Half Cover grants +5 AC; Full Cover prevents you from being targeted by attacks from creatures you have cover from.
  • Concealment & Miss Chance: Concealment grants +5 to Sneak checks and a 10% miss chance on attacks against you. Miss chances do not stack; apply only the highest percentage.
  • Round down after any division.
  • Effects with the same name do not stack. Use the stronger one. Rings are the exception.
  • Advantage and disadvantage: roll two d20s. Advantage keeps the higher roll, disadvantage the lower. They do not stack. A roll with both is a normal roll.
  • Flanking: two attackers flank a target when both are in melee with it and attack from different sides. Each gains +2 to attack it.
  • Vulnerability doubles damage. Resistance subtracts its number from the damage. See Damage Types for the order.
  • Dual Wielding: a creature wielding two weapons has −1 to its Speed. After a regular attack, it makes one more attack with its off-hand weapon for 1 AP, dealing half its rolled damage. The off-hand weapon must be light.
  • Initiative = d20 + Wits modifier. The highest result acts first.
  • Reactions: each creature has 1 reaction per round, refreshed at the start of its turn. Opportunity attacks use it.

Movement

  • Falling Damage: starts at 1d4 bludgeoning damage for 1 meter and doubles for every additional meter fallen (2d4 at 2 m, 4d4 at 3 m, and so on).
  • Holding Breath: a creature can hold its breath underwater for a number of minutes equal to its Fortitude modifier (minimum 1 minute).
  • Jumping (3 AP): jump a number of meters equal to your Speed or half your Strength score (rounded down), whichever is lower.
  • Mounted Combat: while riding a mount, use the mount's Speed − 1. Mounting or dismounting costs 3 AP.

Speed is measured in meters on the map. Each AP you spend lets you move up to your Speed. A creature with Speed 5 moves up to 30 m by spending all 6 AP. You split an AP's movement across your turn. Example: spend 1 AP, move 3 m, cast a spell, then move the last 2 m before your turn ends.

Each speed is walking, flying, burrowing, or climbing.

  • Walking: movement along the ground, mostly on legs. Some creatures, such as snakes, slither instead.
  • Flying: movement through the air by wings, magic, or other means.
  • Burrowing: digging through dirt, sand, gravel, or similar material. Burrowers do not pass through solid rock or metal unless their entry says so or their Strength score exceeds 40.
  • Climbing: movement up walls and other vertical surfaces.

Action Points

Action Points (AP) measure time within a round. Bigger actions cost more AP. You gain 6 AP at the start of your turn and hold no more than 6. Actions cost 3 AP unless their entry says otherwise. Unspent AP is lost at the end of your turn.

AP carryover: declare the action on your current turn and spend your remaining AP on it. On your next turn, your first action spends the AP needed to finish it.

Any action with a casting time longer than 1 round costs 6 AP on every turn it continues in combat.

General Actions

  • Help (2 AP): one creature gains +5 on its next attack roll or save. Help from several creatures does not stack. Take Help again on your next turn to extend the bonus. Help on a skill check gives +2 instead and follows the Helping rules under Skills.
  • Attack (3 AP): make one attack with a weapon you are wielding.
  • Move: each AP spent moves you up to your Speed. See Movement.
  • Object Interaction (4 AP): use an object. Quick, simple tasks, such as opening a door or pushing a light object, cost 2 AP.
  • Activate Equipment (3 AP): read a scroll, drink a potion, light a candle, or trigger a magic item.

Opportunity Attacks

An opportunity attack (OA) is an attack against a creature who leaves itself open in combat. An OA is one Attack. It costs your reaction, not AP. These provoke an OA:

  • Using Activate Equipment. When the item belongs to another creature, or you use it on another creature, both creatures provoke.
  • Having your maximum AP reduced to 0. You stay open to every OA until it rises.
  • Reloading a ranged weapon, or making a ranged attack while in melee.
  • Using a spell or maneuver in melee, unless it is a touch or melee attack.
  • Moving away from a creature in melee range who sees you.
  • Making a distracting check or action, such as picking a lock, stabilizing a creature, picking up an object, or lifting or moving a heavy object.

Damage Types

Damage applies in this order: vulnerability doubles it, resistance subtracts from it, then armor absorbs it. Leftover damage reduces Health.

Physical

Physical damage has four types: Piercing, Slashing, Bludgeoning, and Necrotic. All four reduce Physical Armor. Every attack and every resistance names one of the four.

True

True damage bypasses Physical Armor and damages Health directly. It keeps its source's type: a true-damage sword strike is Slashing, so Slashing resistance applies. True Armor absorbs it.

Psychic

An attack on the mind. Psychic damage reduces Magical Armor.

Fire

Flame and heat. Fire damage reduces Magical Armor.

Cold

Frost and ice. Cold damage reduces Magical Armor.

Earth

Elemental earth acting on the body, such as acid, crushing stone, or petrification. Earth damage reduces Magical Armor.

Poison

Toxins and venom. Poison damage reduces Magical Armor.

Air

The elements present within storms, such as sound or electricity. Air damage reduces Magical Armor.

Holy, Unholy, and Magic

These three damage types read a target's alignment.

  • Holy: damage from the heavens or a force of good, seen as bright light or white flames. It hits Health directly on Evil targets.
  • Unholy: damage from the underworld or a force of evil, seen as bright blue flames or a deep crimson light. It hits Health directly on Good targets.
  • Magic: pure magical energy, seen as a translucent or unseen force. It hits Health directly on Neutral targets.

Against any other target, each reduces Magical Armor. Alignment damage reads only Good, Neutral, and Evil. Tags such as Lawful, Chaotic, or True change nothing. True Armor shields Health from alignment damage.

Measurements

  • 5 rounds equal 1 minute, so each round lasts 12 seconds.
  • Each square on the map represents 1 meter.

Combat DCs

Combat DCs use your class's primary stat: the stat with the higher bonus your class grants when you level up. Your class's primary stat is also your casting stat.

  • Spell or maneuver save DC = 10 + primary stat modifier + magical bonus − penalties.
  • Spell or maneuver attack = level + primary stat modifier + magical bonus − penalties.
  • Multiclassed characters use the primary stat of the class that grants the spell or maneuver.

Critical and Decisive Strikes

Only physical melee and ranged attacks roll for Critical Strikes. With the Deadly Magic feat, melee and ranged spell attacks roll for them too.

When an attack allowing Critical Strikes hits, roll a percentile die (d100). On a result at or under your Critical Chance (your Wits score), the attack becomes a Critical Strike and doubles all rolled damage dice.

A natural 20 on the attack roll is an automatic Critical Strike. Roll the percentile die again. On a result at or under your Critical Chance, it becomes a Decisive Strike: the target becomes Crippled, and all rolled damage dice are tripled instead of doubled.

Armor

  • Armor Penalty: subtracted from all Finesse- and Strength-based skill checks while wearing armor or carrying a shield with a penalty.
  • Unproficient Armor: wearing armor you are not proficient in incurs a 20% miss chance on all attack rolls and doubles the armor's Armor Penalty.

Armor represents a creature's resistance to types of damage, built up through its past.

  • Physical Armor is a creature's ability to resist cuts, stabs, and blunt force. Warriors who train their bodies grow resilient to physical attacks. Physical Armor = 5 × Fortitude modifier (minimum 0).
  • Magical Armor measures a creature's understanding of magic, which is how it defends against lasting damage from non-physical effects, even those not caused by magic, such as heat, electricity, and poison. Magical Armor = 5 × Intelligence modifier (minimum 0).
  • True Armor is rare and comes only from a few features. It shields Health from true damage and from alignment damage bypassing armor. Other damage reaches it only after Physical and Magical Armor.

Armor returns only through rests, like Health.

Monsters do not have separate Physical and Magical Armor. They have a single armor value and possibly a True Armor value. Anything affecting Physical or Magical Armor affects a monster's armor value as if it were that armor.

Armor Class

Armor Class = your worn armor's AC + Finesse modifier, up to the armor's maximum Finesse bonus. With no armor, AC = 10 + Finesse modifier.

A shield adds its AC to your Armor Class.

Vision Types

  • Darkness: a creature without True Vision or Dark Sight cannot see in darkness. It takes −10 on all Sense Environment checks relying on sight while in darkness.
  • True Darkness: a creature without True Vision or Dark Sight is Blind in true darkness. A creature with Dark Sight but not True Vision takes −10 on Sense Environment checks relying on sight.
  • Witch Sight: a creature with Witch Sight sees through illusions and even physical barriers created by magic. It sees through them and centers spells on the opposite side of the wall, but spells traveling through space interact with the barrier as normal.
  • Supernatural Vision: a creature with Supernatural Vision sees through true darkness and identifies shapechangers. It does not see their true form, but the creature appears twisted and unnatural. It also sees into the border world.
  • True Vision: a creature with True Vision sees through shapechangers and sees their true form if it chooses. It also sees through illusions and true darkness.
  • Dark Sight: a creature with Dark Sight sees through darkness as if it were bright light. Dark Sight does not see through true darkness.
  • Blind Sight: used by creatures perceiving their environment without eyes. It is mostly sound-based, as with bats, but takes other forms, such as smell. A creature with Blind Sight does not perceive things requiring sight, such as color.

Resting

Resting fills the time between strenuous activities such as combat, travel, or casting spells. During a rest, a creature does only light activity, such as reading, writing, or sleeping. Armor and Health return only through rests.

  • Partial Rest (8 hours): you regain Mana and Stamina equal to your level, Physical Armor equal to your Fortitude score, and Magical Armor equal to your Intelligence score.
  • Full Rest (24 hours): you restore Health, Mana, Stamina, and armor to maximum. You level up if you meet the requirements. Abilities used "per full rest" refresh.
